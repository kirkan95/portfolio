## Tier 1 — Best Overall Fit

### 1. Developer Advocate / Developer Relations

**What it is:** You represent a technical product to its developer audience — writing tutorials, building demos, giving talks, shaping docs, and feeding real user insight back to the engineering team. The job is 40% communication, 40% code, 20% community.

**Why Kirk fits:**
- Journalism training = rare written communication clarity in a role flooded with engineers who can't explain things
- Has built complex systems AND written about them for non-technical audiences (editors, reporters)
- Agentic AI systems (City Council monitor, recipe database) are exactly the kind of demo-worthy work DevRel roles showcase
- Self-initiating builder profile is a strong signal in DevRel hiring

**Salary:** $135k–$220k (remote average ~$175k)
**WLB:** ★ — async, remote-first, low on-call pressure at most companies
**Remote:** ★ — 70%+ of roles are fully remote
**Creative:** ★ — demos, content, talks, product influence
**Pay:** ★

**Best targets:** AI tooling companies (Anthropic, Cursor, Vercel, Supabase, Observable, Vite, Mapbox), data platforms (Databricks, Grafana, Sigma), journalism-adjacent SaaS (Arc XP, Piano)

**Honest tradeoff:** Conference travel can be significant (2-4x/year). Companies sometimes cut DevRel first in downturns. Pick a product you actually care about.

### 2. AI Product Manager — Internal Tools or Content Platforms

**What it is:** You own the roadmap for AI-powered tooling — either internal (helping non-technical teams use AI) or content-facing (AI-powered user products). Four sub-types: copilot PM, platform PM, ML feature PM, AI ops PM.

**Why Kirk fits:**
- Already did this: recipe database AI product (unsolicited, solo-built, shipped to production)
- City Council Monitor is an AI ops PM project built in code
- Quiz system built with stakeholder interviews, data lifecycle design, and non-technical editor UX
- Journalism background means rare editorial PM instinct — can write the PRD *and* the user story *and* the product copy
- Knows what reporters and editors actually need, which is the gap most media PMs can't bridge

**Salary:** $141k–$197k (AI PM, remote); top end $250k–$390k total comp at AI-native companies
**WLB:** ◐ — depends heavily on company and stage. Established company = good. Startup = not good with a newborn
**Remote:** ★ — majority of AI PM roles are remote
**Creative:** ◐ — creative in vision/strategy, but day-to-day is roadmap/meetings
**Pay:** ★★

**Best targets:** Newsroom tech companies (Arc XP, Chorus/Vox Media, Newspack), investigative media with AI teams (The Atlantic, ProPublica), general AI tooling companies building for content/creative industries

**Honest tradeoff:** PM roles attract a lot of competition and can be highly political. Requires shifting from "I build it" to "I decide what gets built." Worth assessing if that suits you.

### 3. Investigative/Nonprofit Newsroom Developer

**What it is:** Senior developer embedded in a nonprofit investigative newsroom — ProPublica, The Marshall Project, The Markup, CalMatters, APM Research Lab, Axios, The 19th. Builds data pipelines, interactives, and AI tools for public-interest journalism.

**Why Kirk fits:**
- Direct skill and credential match — nationally award-winning, full-stack, data viz, pipeline automation
- Award pedigree (ONA, Murrow, IRE) carries weight in investigative circles
- Journalism degree + editorial instinct is a genuine differentiator over pure engineers
- AAJA mentorship signals alignment with the mission-driven culture at nonprofits

**Salary:** $110k–$165k (ProPublica/top-tier nonprofits hit the high end)
**WLB:** ★ — nonprofits are typically 40-hour cultures, flexible, no on-call
**Remote:** ★ — most investigative nonprofits are fully remote or remote-first
**Creative:** ★ — most meaningful and mission-aligned creative work available
**Pay:** ◐ — solid but lower ceiling than private sector

**Honest tradeoff:** Lower salary ceiling. Hiring windows are infrequent — you need to watch job boards and move fast when roles open. Competition is stiff from within journalism.

## Tier 3 — Longer Bets / Worth Watching

### 7. Civic Tech / Government Digital Services

Roles at USDS, Code for America, state digital services offices, or city innovation teams. Mission-aligned, excellent WLB, flexible. Salary typically $90k–$140k — lower ceiling, but some orgs (USDS, 18F successor) can reach higher. Remote availability varies. Worth considering if mission matters more than pay ceiling.

### 8. Technical Curriculum Developer / Learning Experience Engineer

Companies like Codecademy, O'Reilly, Coursera, The Pudding, or journalism schools (Knight, Medill). Journalism + mentorship (AAJA) + technical depth = rare fit. Excellent WLB. Salary ceiling ~$130k. Lower pay upside but genuinely creative and low-stress.

### 9. Freelance / Consulting (Independent Practice)

Data viz and interactive journalism consulting. The Pudding model — high-profile creative data journalism pieces combined with contract work for publishers, NGOs, or brands. Flexible, creative, but income is variable and health insurance is self-managed (stressful with a newborn). Best as a side track until you've built a client base.
